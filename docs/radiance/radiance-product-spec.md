# Radiance Cafe Latte prototype product specification

**Revision 0.1 | 20 October 2026 | Development proposal**

Radiance is a daily Cafe Latte protein concept for middle-aged and older men and women living with diabetes who have an identified dietary protein shortfall. This specification defines four complete dry-blend prototypes, a preparation and evaluation plan, and estimated packed production costs including the selected ivory and champagne-gold tin direction. R1 is the dairy sensory benchmark; R2 tests a lower-cost dairy hybrid; R3 and R4 explore dairy-free routes.

The formulas are starting recipes for a food technologist, not released manufacturing instructions or validated patient nutrition. Ingredient grade selection, category permissions, nutrition, suitability and shelf life must be established before commercial use. Nurture's formulation records remain separate.

The base estimates below include flavour, manufacturing, testing and premium packaging at 1,000 tins per route. The first-launch column additionally recovers INR345,000 of proposed development and packaging setup from the first 1,000 tins of a single eventual winner. R4 depends on a suitable yeast grade and its price; none of these are binding quotations.

## Four complete prototype formulas

- **R1 MPC dairy:** 100% of main protein from MPC80. The reference for milky flavour, body and daily drinking experience.
- **R2 MPC and pea:** 70% of main protein from MPC80 and 30% from pea protein. Tests how much dairy character survives a cost reduction.
- **R3 pea and rice:** 70% of main protein from pea and 30% from rice. The plant-only cost benchmark.
- **R4 pea and yeast:** 70% of main protein from pea and 30% from purified food-grade yeast protein. Both formula suitability and its estimated price are conditional on selection of an exact grade.

Ratios describe delivered main protein. Ingredient mass ratios happen to match because this comparison assumes 80% protein for all four raw materials. Minor ingredients are conservatively counted as zero protein for this calculation, not declared protein-free.

### Ingredient requirements

**Protein powders:** request current signed technical data sheets and lot certificates of analysis. Specify protein as sold, moisture, carbohydrate and sugars, fat, sodium, calcium, potassium, phosphorus, amino acids, allergens, contaminants and microbiological suitability. Include the intended concentration and reconstitution method in the supplier brief. Pea/rice grades need particular attention to particle size, sediment, bitterness and mouth drying. Research on pea beverages supports testing these sensory issues directly. [Pea sensory study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7466195/)

**Yeast:** use a purified, inactive food-grade protein ingredient; ordinary baking yeast, yeast extract and nutritional yeast flakes are not interchangeable with this formula. Obtain the exact grade, organism/process information, protein method, amino acid evidence, nucleic-acid/purine specification and Indian-use documentation. Angel describes S80-A as a soluble beverage grade; that manufacturer description does not establish performance in Radiance. The INR899/kg retail supplier listing does not identify it as S80-A. [AngeoPro listing](https://easybuyingredients.com/product/angeopro-yeast-protein-angel-yeast/), [manufacturer grade description](https://en.angelyeast.com/news/yeast-protein/angel-yeast-at-vitafoods-europe-2026.html)

**Fibre:** digestion-resistant dextrin powder, provisionally at least 90% dietary fibre as sold using an appropriate validated method; not ordinary maltodextrin or an unspecified dextrin. The proposed ingredient quantities contribute approximately 3.150 g fibre in R1, 3.015 g in R2 and 2.835 g in R3/R4 before other ingredients. The market listing used for price context specifies over 85%, so it is not an approved substitute for the proposed 90% grade. Keep the chosen dose subject to digestive-tolerance testing. [Supplier specification context](https://www.rajvigroup.net/nutraceuticals-ingredients.html)

**Creaminess:** use a microencapsulated high oleic sunflower oil powder with nominal 70% fat. At 3 g it contributes about 2.1 g fat, before other ingredients. Require a dairy-free carrier for R3/R4 and inspect the complete carrier/additive specification for every route. A technical example, HOS 70 ND, uses maltodextrin; its carrier contributes to digestible carbohydrate and cannot be ignored. INR1,200/kg is a planning allowance, not a quote for that branded grade. [Manufacturer technical example](https://www.abiteccorp.com/en/product-repository/nutri-sperse-powders/)

**Coffee and flavour:** use unsweetened, genuinely soluble decaffeinated coffee rather than ground coffee or vending premix. At 1 g, caffeine in mg is `supplier caffeine fraction x 1000`; decaf does not imply zero caffeine. The flavour row is one supplier-standardised Cafe Latte compound including cream/vanilla notes, masking components and all carriers. Request a defined grade and its recommended dose before bench work. R1 starts at 0.65 g, R2 at 0.80 g and the dairy-free routes at 1.00 g to budget for greater masking; the extra flavour replaces fibre, preserving serving mass and protein. These are starting doses, not an established optimum. [Soluble decaf candidate](https://mediocon.com/decaffeinated-soluble-coffee-powder-manufacturer-supplier-in-india-mediocon-inc)

**Sweetener:** 0.120 g refers to a professionally prepared **10% w/w sucralose preblend**, delivering **12 mg active sucralose**, not 120 mg and not 0.120 g neat sucralose. Use a specified resistant-dextrin carrier or another documented suitable carrier, counting the whole preblend in the recipe and carbohydrate assessment. Confirm permission, maximum level, calculation basis and declarations for the actual category before a tasting batch. Do not substitute a tabletop sweetener or different potency gram for gram. At the proposed portion the active concentration is about 353 mg/kg powder; prepared-drink concentration depends on the measured final mass. If the category or preferred sweetener requires a change, revise the formula and cost before use.

**Lecithin and salt:** 0.20 g dispersible powdered sunflower lecithin and 0.03 g fine salt per serving are starting functional doses. Added lecithin is not proof of instantisation. Exclude gums, digestive enzymes, botanicals, chromium and added vitamin/mineral premix from this first comparison. Assess B12/D or other fortification later only if there is a nutritional rationale, permitted forms/doses and a qualified premix specification; repeat decisive tests with the complete fortified product.

### Nutrition and allergen development targets

Aim for about 20 g measured protein per portion, roughly 3 g fibre and no unnecessary added sugars. Use a provisional digestible-carbohydrate design ceiling of 5 g per portion in water, subject to actual grade composition; this is not a legal low-carbohydrate claim. Total energy, total sugars, lactose, fat, saturated fat and minerals remain to be calculated from signed ingredient data and confirmed analytically. There is no approved sugar-free, low-GI, glucose-control or diabetes-treatment claim.

R1 and R2 contain milk. R3 and R4 are intended to avoid dairy ingredients, but the fat powder, flavour, sweetener carrier, manufacturing cross-contact and complete supply chain must support any vegan or dairy-free declaration. No route is labelled allergen-free by this proposal. Changing the preparation liquid changes the nutrients, carbohydrates and allergens in the complete serving.

1. Confirm ingredient identity, category permissions and food-safety suitability with the technologist before preparing tasting samples. Record each grade, batch, potency and actual weight. Dry blending provides no microbial kill step, so suitable ingredients and hygienic processing are essential.
2. Gently deagglomerate permitted bulk ingredients following the supplier guidance. Geometrically preblend salt, sweetener preblend, flavour and lecithin into a weighed portion of the fibre/protein. Keep this material within the stated formula mass.
3. Combine the remaining protein, fibre and fat powder in a suitable dry blender. Incorporate the minor-ingredient preblend and coffee with an agreed sequence. Establish blending time and load by uniformity evidence; do not assume an arbitrary duration proves homogeneity.
4. Sample multiple blender positions and beginning/middle/end filling. Check segregation, protein/sweetener uniformity as appropriate and weight variation. Colour or appearance alone cannot validate uniformity.
5. Fill identified trial portions into sealed barrier packs, retain powder and ingredient samples, and record storage conditions. Production would additionally need validated foreign-body controls, line clearance, allergen controls and package-seal checks.

If the selected as-sold protein fraction is P, main ingredient mass is `required protein contribution / P`. Keep the intended 70:30 protein split for hybrids. Recalculate fibre, serving mass, costs and preparation concentration together; do not silently reduce functional ingredients to conceal a protein shortfall.

## Preparation and selection plan

Primary development preparation is 34.00 g in 225 mL water in an open cup. Compare room-temperature water and water initially at 60, 75 and 85 degrees C, three independent cups per condition per route. Add gradually while spoon-stirring for 30 seconds; record temperature after mixing. These are preparation temperatures, not temperatures at which participants should drink. Never shake hot liquid in a closed shaker.

Record wetting, dry-centred lumps, foam, graininess, mouth drying, oil separation and sediment immediately, at 5 minutes and at 15 minutes. If direct mixing fails, record a separate slurry trial using 30 mL room-temperature water plus 195 mL hot water; a slurry-only result does not validate direct hot mixing. Dairy protein hydration is a separate functional problem from a protein-content calculation. [Dairy hydration guidance](https://cdr.wisc.edu/dairy-ingredients-faqs)

Use provisional internal selection gates: no dry-centred lumps after the defined mixing method, no visible curdling or oil separation during 15 minutes, no unacceptable gritty final sip, and acceptable first/middle/last-sip flavour. After ingredient and clinical review, compare blinded cups at equal comfortable serving temperature with the intended audience. Aim initially for mean liking at least 7/9 and at least 70% sweetness/body ratings of just right; these are exploratory commercial targets, not regulatory standards or proof of clinical benefit. Test repeated consumption and digestive tolerance, not only one sip.

For the initial taste trial keep protein, portion, coffee and sweetener dose fixed and record the route-specific flavour doses. Optimise sweetness and creaminess only after the first comparison. Retest selected dairy routes with the intended milk preparation and selected dairy-free routes with an appropriate unsweetened plant beverage, counting its extra nutrients. Any glycaemic-response or efficacy study needs a separately designed clinically supervised protocol, appropriate ethics review and its own budget; consumer tasting or informal glucose readings cannot substantiate clinical claims.

Before launch, establish finished-product nutrition, microbiological compliance, contaminants, uniformity, packaging migration/seal performance, caking, oxidation and unopened/opened stability in relevant Indian storage conditions. Set shelf life and after-opening instructions from results. A fresh cup or an ingredient shelf-life statement does not establish finished-product shelf life.

## Premium tin packaging specification and allowance

Retain the selected Radiance lettering, warm ivory texture, champagne-gold lid, sunburst and engraved Cafe Latte artwork. For the initial small run, specify a decorative cylindrical tin with a resealable high-barrier food-contact pouch inside it. The tin provides the premium appearance and reuse; the validated pouch provides the primary moisture/oxygen barrier. A decorative slip lid alone is not an established hermetic closure. A direct-fill coated/seamed tin with peel seal would be a separate packaging and filling quote, replacing the pouch architecture.

The calculation uses:

```text
servings per tin = 510 / 34 = 15
ingredient charge per portion = sum(ingredient grams x INR/kg / 1000)
fill factor = (510 + 2) / 510
ingredients consumed per portion = ingredient charge x fill factor / (1 - process loss)
packaging sets = ceil(saleable tins / (1 - empty-pack reject fraction))
packaging per portion = sets x complete-set cost / saleable tins / servings per tin
conversion per portion = 34 / 1000 x fill factor x conversion INR/kg
routine testing per portion = batch test allowance / saleable portions
recurring cost = (ingredients consumed + packaging + conversion + testing + inbound/storage) x (1 + contingency)
```

### Interpreting recurring and first launch costs

Recurring costs include consumed ingredients, losses, full packaging, manufacturing/filling, routine release testing, inbound/storage allowance and contingency. They exclude recoverable GST, outbound fulfilment, distributor/platform margins, payment fees, marketing, founder/business overhead and profit. Unrecoverable taxes or duties must be added; final product GST/HSN classification has not been assigned. These numbers are not an MRP or a supplier commitment.

The first-launch column adds INR45,000 packaging setup for one selected SKU and an **INR300,000 shared four-prototype development programme**, recovered entirely from the first 1,000 tins of the eventual winner: `(45,000 + 300,000) / 15,000 = INR23.00` per portion. That is conservative first-run recovery, not recurring production cost and not four duplicate development budgets. The development allowance needs a scoped quote for formulation, sensory work, regulatory review and initial analytical/stability work; specialist process development, novel-ingredient approvals and clinical efficacy studies are additional. At 10,000 winner tins, the same INR345,000 would allocate INR2.30 per portion before any new expenditure.

At the base loss assumption, 512 kg of actual fill requires approximately 522.449 kg raw blend, including expected processing loss. These consumption costs do not represent the cash required to buy every supplier's bag/MOQ multiples. Retained surplus ingredient stock is inventory, not automatically a charge to every consumed serving. Four experimental 1 kg batches have a different, higher cost per usable portion; the production estimate is conditional on a successful route and the stated scale.

Diabetes-directed positioning may change the applicable category and evidence requirements.
