# Nurture MPC coffee trial formulation and testing plan

**Date:** 19 October 2026  
**Stage:** Food-technologist bench trial  
**Audience:** Women aged 25–45; one morning beverage occasion  
**Development target:** At least 15 g measured protein from the powder, enjoyable coffee flavour, preparation in both hot water and hot milk, and approximately INR45 recurring packed cost per serving.

Start with a small MPC-led dairy trial. The proposed 25 g serving contains approximately 15.25 g calculated protein before counting minor contributions from other ingredients. MPC already contains casein and whey; no separate WPC, plant or yeast protein is used in this baseline. The first decision is whether a suitable MPC grade can deliver the desired cup with practical spoon mixing.

This is a formulation for controlled development, not a released recipe. The food technologist must confirm the actual ingredient grades, premix potency, category permissions and preparation method before a supervised tasting. The formulation and test results remain separate from the existing packaging artwork and website claims.

## 1 Trial formulation

Make **1 kg, equivalent to 40 nominal servings**, using the following ingredient specifications. Use weights rather than scoop volumes.

| Ingredient | Per 25 g serving | Per 1 kg bench batch | Intended function |
| --- | ---: | ---: | --- |
| MPC80, selected for rapid beverage hydration | 17.750 g | 710.0 g | Main protein source and dairy body |
| Skim milk powder, unfortified, nominal 35% protein | 3.000 g | 120.0 g | Dairy flavour and some protein |
| Instant coffee, unsweetened, soluble | 1.500 g | 60.0 g | Coffee flavour; caffeine depends on the grade |
| Instant dairy-fat powder, nominal 70% fat | 1.900 g | 76.0 g | Creaminess in water preparation |
| Water-dispersible vanilla/latte flavour powder | 0.300 g | 12.0 g | Aroma balance and masking |
| Powdered sunflower lecithin, dispersible grade | 0.150 g | 6.0 g | Trial wetting/emulsification aid |
| Fine food-grade salt | 0.030 g | 1.2 g | Flavour balance |
| Standardised 10% w/w sucralose preblend | 0.120 g | 4.8 g | Supplies 12 mg active sucralose per serving |
| Custom B12 and vitamin D premix at the specified potency | 0.250 g | 10.0 g | Supplies the provisional added nutrient targets below |
| **Total** | **25.000 g** | **1,000.0 g** | |

### Ingredient selection

- **MPC:** obtain current batch protein values on an as-sold basis, lactose, minerals, moisture, particle/instantisation details, and hydration instructions. A published EnNutrica Formula 8+ sheet lists nominal protein of at least 80%, lactose below 8%, a milky sensory character and an insolubility index; it does not establish this coffee formula's spoon-mixing performance. Its preparation and storage specifications must be checked against the supplied current grade. [1]
- **Fat powder:** use an unsweetened emulsified dairy-fat ingredient with a full specification, including its carrier. A generic vending creamer containing significant glucose syrup or maltodextrin is not an equivalent substitution. Check carbohydrates, fat, protein, oxidation and dispersibility before purchase. Its own protein is not counted in the conservative calculation below.
- **Flavour:** 0.3 g is a grade-dependent starting dose. Use the flavour house's beverage guidance. Account for its carrier and any sweetener already included.
- **Sweetener:** 0.12 g is the weight of a **10% preblend**, not pure sucralose. Prefer a professionally prepared preblend using a suitable dairy carrier. Do not replace it gram-for-gram with a different-potency product or a retail tabletop sweetener. Category-specific permissions, dilution basis and declarations must be confirmed for the final product. [5]
- **Lecithin:** evaluate its effect on the actual blend. Dry-added lecithin does not establish instantisation or solve slow MPC hydration. Gum is deliberately absent from the baseline so that viscosity does not hide a hydration problem.
- **Allergens:** the formula contains milk. Check cross-contact and carrier allergens for every supplied ingredient.

### Protein calculation

Assumed as-sold protein fractions: MPC 0.80 and skim milk powder 0.35.

`17.750 × 0.80 + 3.000 × 0.35 = 15.250 g protein per nominal serving`

This is a conservative spreadsheet estimate, not a validated label value. The extra 0.25 g is not an established manufacturing or analytical tolerance. Verify finished-product protein, serving-weight variation and shelf-life compliance before claiming 15 g.

If the actual MPC protein fraction is `P`, calculate the required MPC mass as:

`MPC grams = (15.25 − protein grams from all other ingredients) ÷ P`

If changing the MPC mass, record the new serving mass and recalculate the full formula. Do not silently reduce another functional ingredient merely to preserve 25 g. Confirm as-sold versus dry-basis protein and the applicable nitrogen conversion factors with the laboratory.

## 2 Micronutrient premix request

Use a custom food-grade premix, with a declared **0.250 g inclusion per serving**. Request these **provisional added amounts at initial manufacture**, expressed as active vitamin equivalents rather than ingredient preparation weights:

| Vitamin | Added amount per serving | Delivered per 1 kg finished powder | Starting form for supplier evaluation |
| --- | ---: | ---: | --- |
| Vitamin B12 | 1.1 micrograms | 44 micrograms | Food-grade cyanocobalamin preparation |
| Vitamin D | 7.5 micrograms, equivalent to 300 IU | 300 micrograms, equivalent to 12,000 IU | Food-grade cold-water-dispersible D3 preparation |

For a premix dosed at 250 mg per serving, the corresponding nominal premix potencies are **4.4 micrograms B12/g premix** and **30 micrograms vitamin D/g premix**. These figures describe the required supplier premix potency; they are not instructions to weigh neat vitamins on a kitchen or ordinary production scale.

The added amounts are each half the adult-woman reference values of 2.2 micrograms B12 and 600 IU vitamin D in the ICMR-NIN 2020 summary. This is a development contribution target, not an individual prescription or a claim that every customer is deficient. Natural vitamin contributions from the dairy base must be measured or supported by appropriate data, then added to these amounts for total-intake and compliance assessment. [2]

Use a premix supplier to evaluate permitted forms and sources, carriers, sensory effect, uniformity, retention during hot preparation and packaged shelf life. D3 source documentation also needs to support the intended vegetarian positioning. The FSSAI schedules provide a starting reference for vitamin forms; the applicable current category and amendments must be confirmed. [4]

No nutrient overage is assumed in this first bench calculation. If retention results justify an overage, the supplier and nutrition/regulatory reviewer must calculate it, document total amounts and confirm applicable limits before a revised batch is made. Do not compensate for unknown losses by adding extra vitamins arbitrarily.

**Do not add calcium salts or iron in this first trial.** The MPC base already contributes calcium. For illustration only, the 2,040 mg calcium/100 g powder value in the cited MPC sheet would imply about 362 mg from 17.75 g MPC, before skim milk powder or preparation milk. That is grade-specific arithmetic, not Nurture's declared calcium value. Count native iron and other minerals as well; “no added iron” does not mean iron-free. [1]

For an early unfortified comparison, replace the premix with the same weight of its supplier-provided blank carrier. Once a base is selected, repeat the decisive tests with the **complete fortified formula**. A carrier-only sample does not validate micronutrient mixing or stability.

## 3 Coffee and caffeine

Use 1.5 g instant coffee as the sensory starting point. The working caffeine target remains **40–60 mg per serving**, subject to actual grade selection and measurement.

`Caffeine mg = coffee grams × supplier caffeine fraction × 1,000`

For example, a verified 3% caffeine coffee would contribute 45 mg from 1.5 g. The example is not a specification for an untested coffee. Account for all flavour ingredients that might contain caffeine. If more coffee flavour is wanted without a higher caffeine amount, compare caffeinated/decaf coffee blends or an appropriate flavour system, with costs and sensory results recorded.

## 4 Bench batching

1. Record ingredient name, grade, supplier, lot, current COA, expiry, weight and declared potency. Confirm the preparation is appropriate for supervised tasting and the formula's intended category. Use potable water and correctly handled pasteurised/UHT milk for cup testing.
2. Use a clean, dry controlled workspace and calibrated balances suitable for the quantities. Use a milligram-readable balance for the small gram-scale batch ingredients where required by its validated minimum weight. Use the specified premixes for microgram vitamins.
3. Gently break visible agglomerates in the bulk dairy powders according to the technologist's sieve specification. Do not grind coffee or nutrient beadlets. Do not sieve the final micronutrient blend unless recovery and uniformity have been validated.
4. Geometrically preblend the nutrient premix, sweetener preblend, salt and flavour with a portion of the weighed skim milk powder. Add progressively larger portions until evenly distributed. Record blending equipment, fill, time and sequence.
5. Separately combine the remaining MPC, milk powder, fat powder and lecithin. Incorporate the small-ingredient preblend and then coffee under conditions that limit segregation. The technologist must establish adequate mixing rather than assuming a fixed number of minutes proves uniformity.
6. Take identified samples from multiple positions. Retain samples before and after filling to assess segregation. Arrange vitamin-uniformity and finished-product composition testing on the selected process; appearance alone cannot confirm micronutrient uniformity.
7. Weigh 25.00 g trial portions and seal promptly in labelled moisture/oxygen-barrier sample packs. Keep an ingredient retain and a finished-powder retain. Record storage conditions and opening dates. No shelf life is assigned by this procedure.

## 5 Preparation screen

Use **25.00 g powder added to 200 mL liquid**, measured before adding powder. The final volume is greater than 200 mL and should be recorded rather than treated as identical to 200 mL liquid. Keep powder dose unchanged between milk and water.

### Primary preparation method

Place 200 mL liquid in an identical open mug. Measure the liquid temperature immediately before adding powder. Add powder gradually while stirring with a normal teaspoon for **30 seconds**, at an agreed repeatable pace. Avoid a frother or blender in the primary screen. Record temperatures again after mixing and at subsequent observations.

### Test matrix

| Liquid | Preparation temperature | Independent cups | Observations |
| --- | ---: | ---: | --- |
| Water, one recorded source | 60°C | 3 | Immediately after mixing; 5 and 15 minutes |
| Water, same source | 75°C | 3 | Same |
| Water, same source | 85°C | 3 | Same |
| One recorded milk type and brand | 60°C | 3 | Same |
| Same milk | 75°C | 3 | Same |
| Same milk | 85°C | 3 | Same |

This is **18 independent cups**, not 18 servings tasted by each participant. The temperatures are preparation conditions. Allow samples to reach the protocol's comfortable serving temperature before tasting; do not ask participants to drink 75–85°C liquid. Do not shake hot liquid in a sealed container.

Add a **1-minute foam-height reading** to the 0-, 5- and 15-minute observation schedule.

Use the milk type customers are most likely to use as the first milk benchmark; a recorded toned milk is a reasonable Indian development starting point. After screening, repeat the selected preparation with another milk fat level and another realistic water source/hardness. If the practical cup is too concentrated in milk, test 225 and 250 mL at the **same powder dose** and record the dilution tradeoff.

### Backup slurry method

If direct mixing fails, repeat at 75°C with both liquids in triplicate: make a smooth slurry with 25 g powder and 30 mL room-temperature liquid, then add the remaining 170 mL hot liquid gradually while stirring. Record the actual final temperature; the mixture will be cooler than the hot portion.

Treat this as a separate preparation method. Do not report a slurry-only success as proof of direct hot mixing. If neither route is acceptable, compare another MPC grade or supplier co-processed powder before increasing stabiliser or flavour.

## 6 Mixing observations and proposed gates

The following are internal development criteria, not legal standards. Agree the criteria before comparing samples. For defect-intensity ratings use 0 = absent, 1 = barely perceptible, 2 = mild, 3 = moderate, 4 = strong and 5 = severe.

| Measure | What to record | Proposed initial gate |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Wetting and lumps | Floating powder, dry-centred lumps, required additional stirring | No visible dry-centred lumps after the defined 30-second method |
| Texture | Graininess/chalkiness and mouth-drying on 0–5 anchored scales | Mean at or below 1 on each scale; investigate any marked individual failure |
| Sediment | Standardised clear-cup photographs at 0, 5 and 15 minutes; describe any gritty last sip | No obvious compact gritty deposit or unacceptable final-sip texture |
| Separation | Surface oil, flocculation, curds or distinct layers | No visible curdling/flocculation or free oil layer during the 15-minute cup period |
| Foam | Height in mm after mixing, at 1 minute and at 5 minutes | Initial target at most 10 mm after 1 minute; revise only if customers prefer a latte foam |
| Body | Thin/just right/thick; instrument viscosity if available | At least 70% of exploratory target users rate body “just right” in each liquid |
| Preparation robustness | Failures across replicate cups, temperatures and sources | Select a practical temperature window in which both liquids repeatedly pass |

Do not average away an entire failing preparation condition. If 85°C fails but 60–75°C passes, narrow and retest the preparation window before writing instructions. No stove-boiling or prolonged hot-holding claim is implied.

Log pH after reconstitution at a recorded measurement temperature, liquid source, powder/liquid mass, mixing time, operator, ingredient lot, standing time and any corrective action. Investigate pH/mineral interactions rather than adding citrate or other buffering salts without trials. MPC rehydration is a documented technical issue; higher protein concentration alone does not guarantee an instant powder. [3]

## 7 Flavour screening

Keep MPC and milk-powder quantities fixed so protein does not drift. Run one-variable comparisons after the baseline preparation screen.

| Trial | Change from baseline | Nominal portion mass | Question |
| --- | --- | ---: | --- |
| A1 | Baseline | 25.00 g | Does the basic latte cup work? |
| A2 | Increase 10% sweetener preblend from 0.12 to 0.16 g; all other weights fixed | 25.04 g | Does 16 mg rather than 12 mg active sucralose improve whole-cup liking? |
| A3 | Increase coffee from 1.50 to 1.80 g; all other weights fixed | 25.30 g | Does stronger coffee improve flavour without excessive bitterness/caffeine? |

For a lower-sweetness comparison, use 0.08 g preblend (8 mg active) and a 24.96 g portion. These are deliberately recorded portion changes; do not normalise all variants to 25 g by reducing the protein base. Caffeine in A3 must be recalculated and reviewed before tasting.

Begin with A1 at 75°C in both liquids if that temperature passes the preparation screen. Use blinded three-digit codes, balanced sample order and equal sensory serving temperature. Compare the variants separately in water and milk. Limit the number and size of caffeinated tasting samples per session and account for participants' other caffeine intake.

Ask an internal panel to record coffee aroma, dairy character, sweetness, bitterness, body, chalkiness, sweetener aftertaste, first sip, middle sip and last sip. Then compare the same selected base with and without micronutrient premix, using the matched blank carrier control. Record any change in flavour, aroma or physical stability.

## 8 Target customer testing

After ingredient, category and food-safety review, recruit approximately **20–30 women aged 25–45** for exploratory testing. Screen milk allergy, relevant intolerance, caffeine sensitivity and intended-use exclusions. The protocol and portion sizes need qualified review; the initial product is not designed as pregnancy, lactation or clinical nutrition.

First run a blinded cup comparison. Then use a short home-use trial of the selected **fully fortified** formula over 5–7 morning occasions with the proposed preparation method. Each participant uses one defined serving per day, not one per preparation condition. Include both preparation preferences rather than forcing every participant to use an unwanted liquid.

Use these provisional commercial gates:

- Overall liking: mean at least **7/9 in water and milk separately** in the exploratory cup test.
- Sweetness and body: at least **70% “just right”** in each liquid.
- Texture and aftertaste: no recurring gritty/chalky or lingering-sweetener defect that prevents finishing a cup.
- Preparation: at least **90% successful preparation** with the proposed instructions during home use, with all failures logged.
- Habit fit: at least **70% report willingness to replace their usual morning beverage at the stated INR79 price**. Also record actual substitutions during the trial, not only stated intent.

These thresholds are proposed decision rules for a small development study. They do not prove population demand, nutrient benefits or long-term adherence. Record discomfort/tolerance, portion completion, whether an additional tea/coffee was consumed, repeat-use preference and reasons for rejection.

## 9 Cost gate

Re-cost the exact selected ingredient grades and supplier inclusion rates, including all flavour and premix carriers. At the earlier **unconfirmed** MPC listing of INR1,050/kg and the previous allowance structure, the revised baseline is approximately **INR41 per recurring packed serving**. At INR1,250/kg MPC it is approximately **INR44.6**. Actual processing and premix quotes must replace the allowances before confirming an INR45 ceiling.

The recurring budget includes ingredients, assumed 2% material loss, beverage processing, routine batch testing, a refill-pouch allocation, inbound/storage and INR3 contingency. It excludes initial formulation/validation expenditure, outbound fulfilment, customer acquisition, business overhead and recoverable tax. A small 1 kg bench batch will cost more per cup than a commercial production run.

Do not remove the creaminess system, flavour quality, micronutrient testing or required processing simply to force the arithmetic below INR45. If a functional MPC grade costs more, identify the resulting price or formulation tradeoff.
